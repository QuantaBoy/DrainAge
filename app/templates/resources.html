Resources

The wards behind the model. Each card is one ward of the Greater Chennai Corporation: the figures come from the drain survey and the hydraulic model, and the base map opens the GCC / SECON-JBA storm water drain sheet the survey was digitised from.

Data sources, and what each was checked against

Every layer the model runs on is real data from a named source. Each one was cross-checked against a second, independent source before it was used, and where two sources disagree the site reports the disagreement rather than quietly picking one.

  • Terrain DEM

    GEDTM30, 1 arc-second (about 30 m) bare earth, from OpenTopography.

    Checked against Copernicus COP30 over the same area, and an independent elevation point file.

    COP30 sits 1.00 m above GEDTM30 on land (median), the expected gap between a surface model and bare earth. The point file turned out to be ellipsoidal, 92.1 m below sea-level heights, so it is not used as a source: it is what confirmed our heights are above mean sea level.

  • Drainage network 10,240 drains

    The GCC storm water drain survey, 2,219 km with sections, invert levels, ward and zone (Chennai SWD maps, OpenCity).

    Checked against the GCC / SECON-JBA ward base-map sheets it was digitised from, ward by ward.

    196 wards across both sources, 91 carry both, and 90 of those agree. Ward 33's zone disagrees between the sheet and the survey, and ward 35 has a sheet but no drains in the survey. Both are reported, neither is patched.

  • Street mapping 80,206 roads

    OpenStreetMap, with its rivers, canals and nullahs used to keep channels draining in the DEM.

    Checked against OpenStreetMap's own bridge tags, and against depths measured on the DEM with and without the waterways.

    1,011 bridges are left out of flooding, because the DEM under a bridge is the river below it. Burning the 574 mapped waterways into the DEM removes false canal "lakes": 5th Street, Kilpauk drops from 212 cm to 51 cm.

  • Rainfall stands in for IMD Doppler radar

    Open-Meteo: 15-minute precipitation drives the flood forecast, hourly drives the rain-movement map.

    Checked against each other, by summing the 15-minute values into hours.

    The two agree hour for hour, as they should from one provider. The check also caught a clock error, where the 15-minute feed can start behind the real time. An IMD Doppler feed would replace this one function and nothing else.

Not yet checked: no flood depth or timing has been compared with a recorded flood, so the order and timing in which streets flood are the trustworthy output, not exact centimetres. Some surveyed drain sizes also look too small for the land they drain, and ward boundaries are inferred from the nearest surveyed drain.
