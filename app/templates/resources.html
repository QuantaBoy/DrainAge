Resources

The wards behind the model. Each card is one ward of the Greater Chennai Corporation: the figures come from the drain survey and the hydraulic model, and the base map opens the ward's GCC / SECON-JBA storm water drain survey sheet (December 2020).

Data sources, and what each was checked against

Every layer the model runs on is real data from a named source. Each one was cross-checked against a second, independent source before it was used, and where two sources disagree the site reports the disagreement rather than quietly picking one.

  • Terrain DEM

    GEDTM30, 1 arc-second (about 30 m) bare earth, from OpenTopography.

    Checked against Copernicus COP30 over the same area, and an independent elevation point file.

    COP30 sits 1.00 m above GEDTM30 on land (median), the expected gap between a surface model and bare earth. The point file is ellipsoidal: 92.1 m below the DEM, where the EGM2008 geoid is -92.03 m, so it confirms the heights are above mean sea level. Against the ward sheets' surveyed road levels at 4,523 manholes, GEDTM30 stands higher the denser the buildings; that part is taken off, which halves the manhole-to-manhole spread of the error (2.93 m to 1.68 m). OpenStreetMap buildings leave water room only between them, and small deep pits are filled unless a mapped tank or lake is there.

  • Drainage network 10,240 drains

    The GCC storm water drain survey, 2,219 km with sections, invert levels, ward and zone (Chennai SWD maps, OpenCity).

    Checked against the GCC / SECON-JBA ward base-map survey: 7,951 manholes read from the 92 sheets, with invert, road-edge level, width and depth.

    Ward and zone agree in 90 of 91 wards (ward 33 differs; ward 35 has no drains in the survey). The two are separate surveys: at the same manholes their inverts differ by a median 0.63 m, and the CSV's sections are square where the sheets' are not. The sheets' own drawn network - 12,566 drains, 1,010 km, measured sections and inverts, flow direction from the surveyors' arrows - replaces the CSV wherever the sheets reach.

  • Street mapping 80,206 roads

    OpenStreetMap, with its rivers, canals and nullahs used to keep channels draining in the DEM.

    Checked against OpenStreetMap's own bridge tags, and against depths measured on the DEM with and without the waterways.

    1,011 bridges are left out of flooding, because the DEM under a bridge is the river below it. Burning the 574 mapped waterways into the DEM removes false canal "lakes": 5th Street, Kilpauk drops from 212 cm to 51 cm.

  • Rainfall stands in for IMD Doppler radar

    Open-Meteo: 15-minute precipitation drives the flood forecast, hourly drives the rain-movement map.

    Checked against each other, by summing the 15-minute values into hours.

    The two agree hour for hour, as they should from one provider. The check also caught a clock error, where the 15-minute feed can start behind the real time. An IMD Doppler feed would replace this one function and nothing else.

Not yet checked: no flood depth or timing has been compared with a recorded flood, so the order and timing in which streets flood are the trustworthy output, not exact centimetres. Most drain sizes are unverified; the sheets' levels and the DEM differ by an unexplained constant 2.87 m, not applied; and ward boundaries are inferred from the nearest surveyed drain.
