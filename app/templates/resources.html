    Datasets

    Every dataset behind the forecast, and what we did with it

    The flood model runs only on real, named data. Each dataset below was gathered from its source, cleaned or digitised, checked against a second independent source, and then put to one job in the model. Whether each file is on this server is checked live.

    From raw data to forecast

    1. 1

      Gathered

      Government surveys, 92 scanned ward maps, satellite terrain, OpenStreetMap and the city's flood record: 16 datasets and 5 live services.

    2. 2

      Digitised and cleaned

      Ward maps read by OCR, drains joined into one network, the terrain corrected to surveyed road levels, bridges and false "lakes" removed.

    3. 3

      Cross-checked

      Every layer tested against a second, independent source; where two disagree, the disagreement is reported, not hidden.

    4. 4

      Used in the model

      Each dataset has one job: where water runs, what the drains carry, which streets flood, and how the forecast compares with past floods.

    What is still uncertain

    • Exact depth. Where and when streets flood matches the record well; exact centimetres only loosely, because the terrain is 30 m and the recorded depths state no unit.
    • Rivers and tide. The 2015 floods were worsened by the Chembarambakkam release and the rivers overtopping, which this rain-and-drains model does not simulate.
    • Drain sizes outside the 92 ward maps come from the city survey, which records almost every section as square; 798 drains with no size take the typical measured one.
    • A constant 2.87 m between the ward maps' levels and the terrain is unexplained and not applied; it does not change where water runs.
